What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Swapping a water heater looks simple until you count the connections. A gas unit needs a leak-free gas connection with a sediment trap, a vent that is sized and pitched correctly so carbon monoxide goes outside, and combustion air it can actually reach. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps to meet local code. Moving to tankless or a heat pump raises the stakes further, with larger gas lines, new vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
