Tankless units are sophisticated machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater comes to life only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Wauwatosa, WI homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Wauwatosa home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. Only then do we recommend a fix, and we can show you exactly why.

After the fault is pinned down, most repairs are finished in a single visit. We descale heat exchangers with a pump and descaling solution circulated through the service valves, replace worn flow sensors, igniters, flame rods, thermistors, and combustion fans, clean inlet filter screens, and correct venting problems that trip pressure switches. When the real issue is a gas line sized for the old tank heater, we tell you plainly and quote the correct pipe run, because no amount of part swapping will fix a unit that is starved for fuel. If you are fighting a cold-water sandwich, we explain whether it is normal behavior for your model or a sign of a setting, fixture, or recirculation problem we can correct. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • Water dripping from the cabinet or the condensate line
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before

Act Before It Gets Worse

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Wauwatosa Water Heater at (414) 209-3949 for a prompt inspection.

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

Tankless heaters look simple from the outside, but inside is a tightly controlled system. One fault code often has several possible causes, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric tankless units carry their own risk, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

Calling a pro often costs less than trial and error. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors with water moving, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
