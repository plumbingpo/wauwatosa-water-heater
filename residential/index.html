Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Wauwatosa Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing on every job, handling everything from a failed heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

Most water heater failures send small signals well before the tank gives out. Keep an eye out for hot water with a rusty tint, popping noises when the burner fires, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually a thick layer of sediment, a spent anode rod, or a worn dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The serial number on the label usually tells us. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Wauwatosa Homes

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Wauwatosa Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your actual heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment coats the tank floor, forces the burner to run longer, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your equipment's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

Consider maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve ready to open, and that lowers monthly energy bills while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most households, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Sized for Your Household

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one burns fuel keeping extra gallons hot around the clock. Wauwatosa Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means matching the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute against the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

Picking a new water heater comes down to more than tank size. Our technician will compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical service, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a properly sized tank is often the simpler, less expensive option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

No hot water, a pilot that won't stay lit, a tank full of sediment, a weeping relief valve: every one of these problems has its own cause and calls for its own repair. Wauwatosa Water Heater diagnoses the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube sending cold water straight to the tap, or a tankless unit flashing error codes.

The right repair depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems get solved with an expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before they spread. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.
