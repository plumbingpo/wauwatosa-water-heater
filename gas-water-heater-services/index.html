A cold shower from a gas water heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Across Wauwatosa, WI, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If you ever smell gas, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Wauwatosa technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Hot water that runs out faster than it used to
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility

Why Professional Gas Water Heater Services Matters

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, largely because you cannot see the biggest hazards. A fitting that is almost tight can seep gas for days before anyone notices. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically require a permit and an inspection. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

Our technicians approach gas heaters with instruments rather than assumptions. They check thermocouple output in millivolts, read gas pressure at the valve, test draft at the hood, check for CO around the appliance, and leak-test every joint they touch. The result is a repair that targets the real fault and a home that is safer for it. We also give you an honest assessment of the heater itself. A tank past about ten or twelve years with a failing valve or a leak is usually a replacement, and we will say so before you spend money on a repair.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
