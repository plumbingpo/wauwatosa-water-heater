Water heater installation covers more ground than a simple swap. Plenty of Wauwatosa, WI projects start with a finished basement or a new addition that needs its own heater. Other jobs involve moving a unit out of a cramped closet, stepping up to a bigger tank for a growing family, or switching fuels entirely. All of them mean new gas piping, vent routes, circuits, or water lines, planned out before anyone cuts a pipe. We take on installations of every size, from a small point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Every installation starts with sizing and location. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. A gas unit needs a vent path to the outside and enough combustion air. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. Electric tankless units can need a heavy dedicated circuit and the panel capacity behind it. Above finished space or in an attic, the heater needs a drain pan piped somewhere safe. We talk it all through in plain language and then give you a firm price.

On installation day, every step gets verified. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. Electric and heat pump units get a circuit matched to their load. Water lines go in with accessible shutoffs and proper transitions between dissimilar metals, and the relief valve is piped full size to a safe discharge point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Installation

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines must be sized for the total load on the system and tested for leaks. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. The plumbing side needs a correctly rated relief valve and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

A professional installation covers the whole job, not just setting the heater. Sizing by first-hour rating or flow rate, tested gas piping, a vent routed to spec, a circuit matched to the load, and the expansion tank, pan, or straps your code requires are all included. Before we go, gas units are checked for draft and carbon monoxide. The result is an installation that is safe, sized correctly, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, we will tell you directly.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
