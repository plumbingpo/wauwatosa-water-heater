If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. Across Wauwatosa, WI, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

The work is hands-on and specific. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in Wauwatosa

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Homeowners can handle the basics: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest is harder than online videos make it seem. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Anode rods are often seized in place and need significant force, sometimes with a flexible rod for low-clearance spaces. An older T&P valve may not reseat once tested, which leaves you with a drip and a replacement to arrange. And on gas heaters, the burner and vent affect how combustion gases leave the house, which is not something to guess about.

We come ready for the common surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring a trained eye. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, point to future problems, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
